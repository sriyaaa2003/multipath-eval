# v2: better algorithms and correlated failures

The v1 conclusion "no algorithm wins" partly reflected weak baselines: `maxflow` ignores cost and `disjoint` picks arbitrary disjoint paths.
v2 adds five algorithms and a second failure model, on the same 80 networks, 25 demands per network, 3 failure scales.

| new algorithm | what it fixes |
|---|---|
| `mincost_maxflow` | among all maximum flows, the cheapest (v1 `maxflow` ignores cost) |
| `disjoint_mincost` | the k edge-disjoint paths of least total cost (min-cost flow) |
| `ksp_wcmp` | k shortest paths, traffic split in proportion to bottleneck capacity |
| `node_disjoint` | k node-disjoint paths |
| `geo_diverse` | greedy paths that avoid the neighbourhood (150 km) of earlier ones |

Failure models have the **same average link failure probability**; only the correlation differs. `independent` is v1. `regional` knocks out every
link within a random radius (30 to 150 km) of a random point on the network, so nearby links fail together.

| finding (mean over networks, 95% CI by resampling networks) | |
|---|---|
| `mincost_maxflow` vs `maxflow`: same capacity, cost | **-7.1** (-9.2 to -5.6), i.e. 18% cheaper; outage also lower (-0.008) |
| `mincost_maxflow` is first by combined score (equal weights) | 89% of scenarios independent, 91% regional; 52% / 54% of random weight vectors |
| `ksp_wcmp` vs `ksp`: capacity, cost | **+0.70** (+25%), -1.19 |
| `disjoint_mincost` vs `disjoint`: cost, outage | -1.83, -0.0044 |

Negative results, stated plainly:
- **Correlated failures barely change the ranking.** The best algorithm is the same under both failure models in 89% of scenarios (Kendall tau of mean scores 0.64). `disjoint_mincost` has higher outage than `ksp` under independent failures (+0.012, CI 0.007 to 0.018) but the gap vanishes under regional failures (+0.0005, CI -0.0024 to 0.0034).
- **`geo_diverse` did not help**: no better than `ksp` (+0.0011 outage, CI -0.0017 to 0.0040) or `disjoint_mincost` under regional failures. The regional model concentrates failures but 150 km buffers add little over link-disjointness on these networks.
- **The best algorithm cannot be predicted from network size and density**: a depth-3 tree (leave-one-network-out) gets 75% against 80% to 82% for always predicting the most common winner.
- Limits are unchanged: 85% of capacities are sampled, cost and failure probability are proxies, and `mincost_maxflow` winning depends partly on the capacity dimension.

Reproduce: `python scripts/run_v2.py` (about 6 minutes), `python scripts/analyze_v2.py`; tests in `tests/test_v2.py`.
